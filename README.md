# BriefWise DI² — Legal Authority Audit and Output-Control Record

A public archive of source-locked legal-authority audits, citation certificates, doctrine maps, workflow demonstrations, and filing-integrity controls from Grounded DI LLC.

## Overview

This repository contains 17 dated artifacts plus this README. The collection was created on May 8, 2026 and expanded through June 17, 2026; the artifacts preserve development and demonstration records reaching back to June and July 2025. They include certificate PDFs, source-locked audit packages, diagrams, a text-based audit demo, and a methodology article.

BriefWise DI² is presented here as a staged legal-output validation architecture. It organizes facts and procedural posture, resolves authorities against sources, maps propositions to authority, checks quotations and pinpoints, routes uncertainty, and preserves an audit trail before a document is treated as filing-facing. The repository is a public record of those mechanisms and recorded audit milestones, not the complete private runtime or a filing-use certification.

The dated terminology—VaultSeal, CanonLock, Scroll, Gatekeeper, entropy, and FilingReady—is preserved because it is part of the record. The plain-language descriptions below are orientation, not replacements for definitions in the dated artifacts.

## Core control model

The artifacts describe a staged path rather than a one-shot drafting prompt:

```text
facts and jurisdiction / posture
→ authority and source resolution
→ quote, pinpoint, and proposition checks
→ hierarchy and adversarial review
→ audit-trace and final-output comparison
→ filing-use gate or attorney escalation
```

The Level 1 demo makes the same separation explicit for a Pennsylvania asbestos summary-judgment scenario: product presence is not treated as defendant-specific asbestos exposure; uncertainty is logged; and `FilingReady` is `REVIEW_REQUIRED` when deposition, expert, pinpoint, and citator inputs are missing.

## What the record demonstrates

**Governed execution.** The certificate and demo records show rule-oriented gates, source-linked authority review, proposition-to-authority mapping, uncertainty routing, Gatekeeper corrections, and explicit `FilingReady` boundaries.

**Replay and provenance.** The June checkpoint describes a three-document audit package—PDF artifact, Markdown source, and SHA-256 checksum file—with prompt preservation and change detection. The public law-suite package also preserves audit-text / prompt hashes.

**Model capability.** The archive does not benchmark an underlying language model or claim that model generation alone is deterministic. The demonstrated subject is the surrounding audit and control path.

**Artifact status.** These are certificates, audit-building records, diagrams, and demonstrations. They are not a filed brief, legal opinion for a particular client, deployed product, or certification by a court or outside auditor.

**External validation.** The artifacts preserve their own source-check and Gatekeeper results. No independent third-party acceptance of the full system is asserted in this repository.

## Technical significance

A notable strength of the record is that it treats legal-output reliability as a sequence of inspectable checkpoints: authority existence, quote and pinpoint fidelity, proposition support, jurisdiction and posture, hierarchy, final-text traceability, and fail-closed handling for unresolved material. This makes it possible to say exactly which part of an audit passed and which part still blocks filing use, rather than reducing every result to a vague “verified” or “unverified” label.

## Validation and scope

The repository contains no executable BriefWise runtime, package manifest, or test harness. No fresh citation replay was run during this README review. The recorded certificate results remain important evidence of the work performed when those artifacts were created, but they do not substitute for current source access, Shepard’s / KeyCite or equivalent citator review, official-source and reporter verification, exact quote and pinpoint checks, jurisdiction and procedural-posture validation, record-cite support, negative-treatment screening, final deduplication, and attorney authorization.

The June checkpoint expressly marks provisional unique authorities as unresolved and `FilingReady` as not yet determinable. The Level 1 demonstration likewise states that its facts are a structured example and that deposition, expert, and citator inputs were not reviewed. Those are specific boundaries on filing use—not a negation of the recorded audit milestones.

## Evaluation and integration context

Legal-technology teams can evaluate this archive as a reference for source-linked citation quality control, proposition mapping, jurisdiction/posture routing, audit-trace preservation, and human-in-the-loop escalation. A proof of concept could begin with a bounded authority batch and a defined output gate, then compare the preserved audit path with attorney review. The repository itself does not provide filing authorization or legal advice.
